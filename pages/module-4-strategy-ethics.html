AI Upskill · Module 4

Strategy, money and ethics

Where AI pays, where it costs, and where it must not go

Module 4 of 5About 40 minArtefact strategy/business-case.md

A one-page case for one AI change in your work: what it costs, what it saves, who could be harmed, and the rule that stops the harm.

Why it matters

Half the AI projects in Australian businesses this year will be approved because someone was enthusiastic and cancelled because nobody measured anything. The page you write today is the difference between being the person who bought a tool and the person who made a case.

Where the money actually is

Four honest sources of value, in descending order of how often they are real:

SourceLooks likeUsually worth
Time returnedHours a week back from repetitive drafting, sorting, checkingReal, if you name the hours and the person
Quality floorFewer missed details, fewer returns, fewer angry emailsReal, if you can count the returns
Speed to first draftA proposal in twenty minutes instead of two hoursReal for bids; often imaginary for internal work
New capabilitySomething you could not do at all beforeRare, and usually the only one worth a project

The fake ones to name and refuse: "productivity" with no hours attached, "innovation" with no artefact, and any saving that assumes a person's entire salary disappears.

The arithmetic nobody does

A tool that saves four hours a week for one person is roughly $12,000 a year at a loaded rate of $60 an hour. A tool that costs $30 a seat for twelve people is $4,320 a year, before the hours spent learning it, fixing its mistakes and supervising it. Most "obvious" wins are one careful page away from being obvious losses.

Risk, in the language of the people who sign things off

Build the register before someone builds it for you:

ClassExampleControl that satisfies an auditor
Customer dataPasting a client's details into a chat windowWritten rule, redaction habit, approved tools only
Personal informationStaff records, health detailsNot in prompts at all, or a local model
CommercialPricing, contracts, marginsApproved tools only, and no training on your data
Public claimsAnything a customer will readHuman review before publication, always
Decisions about peopleHiring, credit, performanceNo automated decision without appeal and a named human

Two Australian specifics worth knowing without pretending to be a lawyer: the Privacy Act applies to personal information wherever it is processed, and AI-assisted decisions about individuals are already attracting regulatory attention. Your control is the same either way: know where the data went, and be able to show a human decided.

Ethics that are not decoration

  • Disclosure: say when something was drafted with AI, especially to clients and in anything published.
  • Reversibility: prefer the version of the workflow you can undo.
  • Attention: if a person's job becomes supervising a machine 40 hours a week, that is not a saving, it is

a worse job with a spreadsheet attached.

  • The quiet harms: a model that erodes a junior's chance to learn by doing the learning for them.

Your turn

  • Pick one real change, small enough to do this quarter, in strategy/business-case.md
  • Write the value in hours and dollars, with the assumptions visible
  • Write the full cost, including the hours to learn and to supervise
  • Build a risk register of five rows using the classes above, each with a control you would actually follow
  • Write one disclosure rule you will apply to your own output from now on
  • Write the sentence you would say to your boss, in plain English, in under thirty words

The check

The agent reads the case and confirms:

  1. One change, this quarter, with a named person whose hours change.
  2. Value expressed in hours and dollars, assumptions shown, no "productivity" without a number.
  3. Costs include learning and supervision time.
  4. Five risk rows, each mapped to a class and a control that is operational, not aspirational.
  5. A disclosure rule that is specific enough to be broken.
  6. The thirty-word sentence is under thirty words and contains no jargon.

Failure signs: a case that would be approved by anyone, a register with no customer data class, or controls of the form "be careful".

Sealed when

strategy/business-case.md passes the six checks and the value, cost and disclosure rule are recorded in your progress file.
