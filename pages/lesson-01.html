Lesson 1 - Your work, task by task

Module PositionAbout 40 minArtefact position/tasks.mdLesson 1 of 15

Before you start

Answer in a line each, out loud or here, before the new material. Retrieval first, teaching second.

  1. Name the last task you did today that took more than ten minutes.
  2. Which part of it was reading or typing, and which part was deciding?
  3. Who checks your work now, and how do they know it is right?

What you are making

A list of eight to twelve things you actually do in a working week, each labelled by what an AI can carry.

Why it matters

"Will AI take my job" is unanswerable. "Which of my tasks can a machine carry, draft, or never touch" is answerable this afternoon, and it is the only version of the question that changes what you do on Monday.

The demo

The coach writes its own job as a task table: every task it does, one row each, labelled machine-carried, machine-drafted, human-checked, or human only, with a one line reason for each. It says out loud why "deciding what to build" is human only while "drafting the lesson text" is drafted.

Your turn

Write your own table in position/tasks.md, eight to twelve rows. For each row: the task as you would describe it to a friend, how often you do it, the label, and one sentence of evidence from your own week. Rules for the handover:

  • Use real tasks, not job titles.
  • If you cannot say when you last did it, it is not a task you have; cut it.
  • At least one row must be human only, and you must say why.

The check

The coach reads the file and checks:

  1. Eight to twelve rows, each with a frequency and a label.
  2. Every machine-carried row names the machine and the input it would need.
  3. Every human only row gives a reason that is not "it is important to me".
  4. No row is a whole job.

Failure signs: labels with no evidence, or twelve rows that are all the same task reworded.

Sealed when

position/tasks.md passes the four checks and the coach has written it into your progress file.

The standard

Published before you start, so nobody is graded against a standard they have not seen. All rubrics.
